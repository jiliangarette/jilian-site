<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Portfolio depth beats resume length.</strong> Ask for live links, GitHub repos, or case studies with measurable outcomes, not just a list of technologies.</li><li><strong>Rates vary widely by engagement type.</strong> Freelance developers in Singapore typically charge anywhere from S$50 to S$150+ per hour depending on seniority and specialization, while full-time hires command annual salaries well into six figures.</li><li><strong>Offshore teams (including Cebu, Philippines) are a legitimate option</strong> for many Singapore businesses. They offer strong technical talent at lower rates, provided communication and time-zone overlap are managed well.</li><li><strong>A structured interview process</strong> — technical screen, portfolio review, and a paid trial task — reduces mis-hires more than any single resume detail.</li><li><strong>Start with a scoped conversation, not a job ad.</strong> Most successful engagements begin with a short discovery call to define scope before any code is written.</li></ul>
<h2>How to Find a Good Web Developer in Singapore</h2>
<p>If you're asking &quot;how do I find a good web developer in SG,&quot; the honest answer is simple: you don't find them through job boards alone. Singapore's developer market is split across a few channels, and each has different reliability.</p>
<h3>Where Singapore Businesses Actually Source Developers</h3>
<p>Most companies use a mix of these channels rather than relying on one:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Referrals from other founders or CTOs</strong> — the highest-trust source, but limited in volume.</li><li><strong>Freelance platforms</strong> (Upwork, Toptal) — fast to access, but quality varies enormously and you must vet independently.</li><li><strong>Local dev studios and independent consultants</strong> — often the best middle ground for small-to-medium projects, since you get a portfolio and direct accountability.</li><li><strong>LinkedIn outreach and tech communities</strong> (Singapore-based Slack/Telegram groups, ReactJS SG, Geeks on a Beach meetups) — useful for niche skills.</li><li><strong>Offshore partners</strong>, particularly in the Philippines, where cities like Cebu have a growing base of software developers serving Singapore and other APAC clients at a lower cost base.</li></ul>

<h3>What &quot;Good&quot; Actually Means Here</h3>
<p>A good web developer in Singapore isn't just someone who can write clean code. You want someone who:</p>
<ol><li>Can explain technical trade-offs in plain language. This matters more than people expect once you're mid-project and something needs to change.</li><li>Has shipped production applications, not just tutorial projects or bootcamp exercises.</li><li>Understands Singapore-specific requirements where relevant — PDPA compliance for data handling, PayNow/GrabPay integrations for local payments, or hosting considerations for latency-sensitive apps serving the SG market.</li><li>Gives you a realistic timeline and flags risk early, rather than agreeing to everything upfront.</li></ol>
<p>If you want a working example of what a developer portfolio review should look like — real projects, the problem solved, and the stack used — jilian.dev's work page is a useful reference point for the level of detail to expect from any candidate.</p>
<h2>What to Look For: Web Developer Skills That Matter in 2024</h2>
<p>Technical skill requirements shift fast, but a few things have stayed consistent for Singapore-based projects.</p>
<h3>Core Technical Skills</h3>

<h3>Soft Skills You Can't Skip</h3>
<p>Technical ability gets most of the attention. But the projects that go wrong usually fail on communication, not code. Look for:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Responsiveness during the sales conversation</strong> — if a developer is slow to reply before you've paid them, that pattern rarely improves after.</li><li><strong>Willingness to push back</strong> on unrealistic scope or timeline, rather than agreeing to everything to win the job.</li><li><strong>Clear, jargon-free updates</strong> — you should understand project status without needing a translator.</li></ul>

<h2>Developer Portfolio Review: What Separates Strong From Weak</h2>
<p><img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/14936124/pexels-photo-14936124.jpeg?auto=compress&amp;cs=tinysrgb&amp;dpr=2&amp;h=650&amp;w=940" alt="Developer Portfolio Review: What Separates Strong From Weak" loading="lazy" /><em>Photo by Ann H on Pexels (Pexels License)</em></p>
<p>A portfolio review is the single highest-leverage step in hiring a software developer in Singapore, and it's the one most businesses rush.</p>
<h3>What a Strong Portfolio Includes</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Live, working links</strong> — not screenshots. If a &quot;live&quot; project throws errors, that's your answer.</li><li><strong>A clear problem statement</strong> for each project: what the client needed, what was built, and what changed as a result.</li><li><strong>Range</strong>, not repetition — a mix of project types (e-commerce, internal tools, marketing sites) shows adaptability rather than one trick repeated.</li><li><strong>Recent work.</strong> Ask when each project was completed; a portfolio that hasn't updated in two years is a warning sign in a field that moves this fast.</li></ul>
<h3>Questions to Ask During the Review</h3>
<ul><li>&quot;Walk me through a project that didn't go as planned — what happened?&quot; Honest answers here tell you more than any polished case study.</li><li>&quot;What would you do differently on this project if you built it again today?&quot;</li><li>&quot;Can I speak to this client as a reference?&quot; A developer confident in their work will say yes without hesitation.</li></ul>
<p>If you're evaluating candidates and want a benchmark for what a transparent, outcome-focused portfolio looks like, browsing jilian.dev's project work gives a concrete point of comparison before you shortlist.</p>
<h2>Software Development Services: Freelancer, Agency, or In-House?</h2>
<p>This is the decision most Singapore businesses get stuck on. The right answer depends on project length and complexity, not just budget.</p>
<h3>Comparing Your Options</h3>

<p>Independent developers offering direct software development services often sit in a sweet spot for small-to-mid businesses. They bring lower overhead than an agency, more accountability than a large freelance marketplace listing, and faster turnaround on a scoped project.</p>
<h3>Why Cebu, Philippines Comes Up in Singapore Hiring Searches</h3>
<p>Singapore businesses frequently compare local hires against offshore developers. Cebu specifically has built a reputation as a strong secondary tech hub in the Philippines — alongside Manila — with a growing pool of software developers serving international clients, including many in Singapore. A top-rated software developer in Cebu typically offers comparable technical output to a mid-tier Singapore hire at a lower hourly rate. That's why outsourcing there has become common for startups managing runway carefully. The trade-off is coordination: a roughly one-hour time difference between Manila/Cebu and Singapore is manageable, but you still need documented processes and async-friendly communication habits (written specs, recorded demos, shared project boards) to avoid friction.</p>
<h2>Red Flags to Watch For</h2>
<p>Before you commit to hiring a software developer in Singapore or overseas, watch for these warning signs:</p>
<ul><li>Vague or evasive answers about past project outcomes.</li><li>No willingness to do a small paid trial task before a larger commitment.</li><li>Portfolio links that are broken, private, or unexplainable.</li><li>Pressure to sign a long-term contract before any working relationship is established.</li><li>No questions asked about your business goals — a developer only interested in the tech stack, not the outcome, is a warning sign.</li></ul>
<p>According to Singapore's <a href="https://www.imda.gov.sg/">Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)</a>, the tech sector continues to face strong demand for skilled developers. This keeps competition for good talent high and makes careful vetting — rather than speed — the more valuable strategy for hiring businesses.</p>
<h2>FAQs</h2>
<p><strong>How much does it cost to hire a software developer in Singapore</strong>?Costs vary by engagement type. Freelance developers typically charge S$50–S$150 per hour depending on experience, while full-time in-house hires often command annual salaries from roughly S$60,000 up to S$120,000 or more for senior roles. Agency projects are usually quoted per-scope rather than hourly.</p>
<p><strong>Is it better to hire locally in Singapore or outsource to the Philippines</strong>?It depends on project type. Local hires suit projects needing frequent in-person collaboration or same-timezone urgency. Outsourcing to Cebu or Manila often reduces cost by 30–50% and works well for well-specified projects with strong async communication habits already in place.</p>
<p><strong>What should I ask for in a developer portfolio review</strong>?Ask for live links (not screenshots), a plain-language explanation of the problem each project solved, recent work completed within the last year or two, and at least one reference you can contact directly.</p>
<p><strong>What web developer skills matter most for a Singapore-based business</strong>?Frontend frameworks like React or Next.js, backend API design, database structuring, cloud hosting knowledge, and an understanding of PDPA-compliant data handling are the most consistently useful skills for Singapore business projects.</p>
<p><strong>How long does it typically take to hire the right developer</strong>?For freelance or independent hires, a well-run vetting process — portfolio review, technical screen, small paid trial — usually takes one to three weeks. In-house hiring processes generally take longer due to interview rounds and notice periods.</p>
